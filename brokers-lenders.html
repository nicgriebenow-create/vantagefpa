What changed on October 1, 2026

SBA's SOP 50 10 8.1 took effect. For a business expansion or an initial acquisition financed under 7(a), the lender must obtain a quality of earnings report when the business purchase price, measured before buyer equity, seller debt or other financing and excluding owner-occupied real estate, is $3,000,000 or more. Owner buyouts and ESOP or cooperative deals sit outside it. Vantage's read comes before that QoE. It is not the QoE, and it doesn't produce the Cash Proof the SOP asks the QoE to include.

What you already know about your economics

Nothing here is news to anyone who has run a process. It is the reason a numbers partner belongs on your bench before you need one.

You eat only what closes

A success fee is contingent by design. A deal that dies in month eight pays close to nothing for eight months of work, and the calendar it consumed is gone either way.

Diligence is where the price moves

The buyer's team goes into the books and finds figures the records cannot support. From there the process stalls while somebody reconstructs the answer, the price moves, or the seller decides they have been ambushed and walks. It is usually an add-back nobody documented at the time.

The books gap is structural

The back office often grew slower than the company. That is a tooling and process gap, and it is nobody's failure, but the buyer's team will price it anyway.

The lender reads the same package

A lender underwriting the acquisition reads the same package the buyer does. When the add-backs are on their own rows with the evidence attached, the credit memo and the buyer's model are working from the same figures, and the lender's QoE starts from questions that already have answers.

Evidence assembled before it is demanded

Two ways in, depending on which side of your book the deal sits on. Both produce the same artifact, a package where each figure carries the source row that produced it.

Pre-diligence packaging, for your listings

Run before the listing goes to market, or the moment a letter of intent puts a clock on it. The seller sees the hard questions first and answers them on their own schedule, with the documentation attached while the records are still fresh.

  • Add-back positions documented with evidence, one row each
  • Cash tied to the bank by account and month, when bank statements are supplied. This is not the SOP's Cash Proof.
  • The questions a buy-side team typically asks, answered in advance
  • Package ready before the data room opens

The read before the QoE, for buyers and lenders

For the buyer on the other end, for a lender underwriting the financing, or for your own buy-side mandates. Reported to adjusted EBITDA where each adjustment carries its basis and its source, red flags with the evidence attached, and a question set sized against what the analysis actually found. It is not a QoE and does not replace the one the lender obtains.

  • Reported and adjusted EBITDA, with each add-back on its own row
  • Add-backs tied line by line to the general ledger
  • Red flag analysis with source evidence attached
  • Judgment items identified and handed over for you to size

What a re-trade is made of

One add-back, two versions. The item is the same in both columns and so is the seller. The only difference is whether the evidence was assembled before the buyer's team asked for it or after.

How it usually arrives

  1. An owner vehicle and travel add-back appears as one line on a summary schedule
  2. The supporting detail sits in an inbox, a card statement, and somebody's memory
  3. The buyer's analyst asks which accounts it came from and over which months
  4. Reconstruction stalls the process, and the seller is now defending
  5. The buyer discounts what cannot be shown quickly, and the number moves

How it arrives from Vantage

  1. The same add-back sits on its own row
  2. The row names the source account and the months
  3. The basis is written in words, so a reviewer can read it
  4. The buyer's analyst can accept or reject the line without unpicking the schedule
  5. The conversation is about the merits of the item, with the delay gone

How this works between us

Plain terms, in writing, and a lane built to run in both directions. Vantage is the numbers seat on your bench and has no interest in the deal itself.

A referral agreement, papered

For brokers. Send a client and there is a standing referral agreement that pays you a share of collected fees on the engagement, stated in writing before anything moves. The same structure you already work in, a percentage of a transaction you helped cause.

A lane designed to run both ways

The sell-side readiness work is designed to meet founders years before they pick an intermediary. As those relationships develop, the introductions are meant to flow to the brokers who send deals here.

Your client stays your client

Vantage does the numbers work and nothing else. No listing, no buyer introductions, no advice on price or structure, and no opinion on whether to do the deal. The relationship, the process, and the fee are yours, and each engagement is scoped in writing so that stays true.

Never a knock on the seller

The books gap is framed to the client as a tooling problem, because that is what it is. Nobody hears that their bookkeeper failed or that their broker should have caught it. The seller has to stay in the process, and a seller who feels judged does not.
